TOOLS
------------------------
✅ Preferred for physics journals: pdfLaTeX (using LaTeX)
Almost all major physics journals — especially those that publish theoretical and mathematical physics — prefer or require pdfLaTeX, not LuaLaTeX or XeLaTeX.
This includes:

APS journals (PRL, PRD, PRA, PRX)
AIP journals (JAP, Chaos)
IOP journals (CQG, J. Phys. A, EPL)
Elsevier physics journals (Physica A/B/C, Annals of Physics)
Springer physics journals (EPJ series)
arXiv submissions in physics categories

pdfLaTeX is the industry standard for:

reproducibility
consistent fonts across platforms
compatibility with journal production pipelines
automated typesetting systems

⭐ LuaLaTeX and XeLaTeX are allowed but not preferred
Journals usually accept LuaLaTeX/XeLaTeX, but only if:

you need Unicode-heavy text,
you require non-Latin scripts,
or you rely on system fonts.

They are not the default and sometimes introduce compatibility issues:

different microtype behavior
different math font handling
sometimes inconsistent spacing
not always supported in publisher’s final print pipeline

Editors prefer classical LaTeX → pdfLaTeX unless you have a specific reason to use something else.

So which should you use?
✔ Use pdfLaTeX (with lmodern)
This is the most compatible path for your condensate‑gravity theory paper.
It ensures:

submission will compile on the journal’s automated system
PDF output will match what editors expect
RevTeX 4.2 behaves exactly as intended
microtype works (with scalable fonts enabled)
no surprises at production stage

===============================
Typical structure:
-------------------------------
main.tex
figures/ (folder)
bibliography.bib

-------------------------------
Your main.tex will have:
\documentclass[twocolumn]{article}  % or journal template
\usepackage{amsmath, amssymb, graphicx, physics}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
...
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
...

\section{Theory}
...

\section{Results}
...

\section{Conclusion}
...

\bibliographystyle{unsrt}
\bibliography{bibliography}

\end{document}

================================
Use the following headers in the tex file:
--------------------------------
\documentclass[preprint,onecolumn,longbibliography]{revtex4-2}

% --- scalable fonts for pdfLaTeX ---
\usepackage{lmodern}           % essential for scalable Type1 fonts
\usepackage[T1]{fontenc}
\usepackage[utf8]{inputenc}

% --- core math & graphics ---
\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb,amsfonts}
\usepackage{bm}
\usepackage{physics}
\usepackage{siunitx}
\usepackage{graphicx}
\usepackage{xcolor}

% --- micro-typography (stable settings for MiKTeX) ---
\usepackage[final,tracking=true,expansion=false]{microtype}

% --- hyperlinks & refs ---
\usepackage[hidelinks]{hyperref}
\usepackage[nameinlink,capitalize]{cleveref}
