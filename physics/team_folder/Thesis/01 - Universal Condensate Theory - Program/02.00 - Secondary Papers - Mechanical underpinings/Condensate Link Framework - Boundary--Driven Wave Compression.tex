\begin{abstract}
We develop a microphysically explicit realization of the \emph{Condensate Link Framework} (CLF), in which gravity emerges as the geometric response (``stiffness'') of a quantum condensate vacuum matched to its mechanical stiffness. We identify \emph{Condensate Formation with Boundary--Driven Wave Compression} (CF--BWC) as the physical engine that provides a short--lived, early--Universe increase in gravitational stiffness. As a proto--vacuum fluid converts to a coherent vacuum condensate, the advancing phase boundary compresses internal waves (excitations), temporarily amplifying short--wavelength fluctuation content. Through the well--known one--loop induced gravity mechanism, this enhancement raises the effective Einstein--Hilbert prefactor within a narrow epoch, boosting the early expansion rate, shrinking the sound horizon, and thereby increasing the CMB--inferred $H_0$ (an EDE--like effect), while naturally decaying to respect BBN, CMB, late--time, and GW170817 constraints. This completes the CLF by supplying the missing microphysics that ties mechanical stiffness to a cosmologically testable, transient modification of the curvature response.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Two facts motivate the present construction. First, quantum fields in curved spacetime radiatively generate an Einstein--Hilbert (EH) term in the one--loop effective action (``induced gravity'') \cite{Sakharov1967,Vassilevich2003,Schwinger1951}. Second, CMB--inferred cosmology can return a larger $H_0$ if the pre--recombination expansion is very briefly enhanced, shrinking the sound horizon (the logic behind Early Dark Energy, EDE) \cite{EDEReview2023,PoulinReview2023,Planck2018}. The \emph{Condensate Link Framework} (CLF) posits that gravity's strength is the effective stiffness of a vacuum condensate matched to its mechanical properties. Here we provide the missing microphysics: a moving phase boundary during condensate formation \emph{compresses waves} in the proto--fluid, transiently amplifying fluctuations and hence the induced curvature coefficient. The result is a narrow, early boost to $H(a)$ that leaves late cosmology intact and passes known constraints \cite{RiessReview2024,GW170817,Alvey2020}.

\section{The Condensate Link Framework (CLF): Recap}
The CLF asserts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The vacuum is a coherent quantum condensate; gravity is the \emph{geometric stiffness} that matches the condensate's \emph{mechanical stiffness}.
\item The EH term $\int \sqrt{-g}\,R$ is not fundamental but \emph{induced} by integrating out fluctuations, with an effective coefficient $M_{\rm ind}^2$ that depends on the fluctuation spectrum \cite{Sakharov1967,Vassilevich2003}.
\item Cosmological parameters---in particular, the strength of gravity and its early--time behavior---can be linked to the condensate's microphysics via a mechanical--to--geometric matching.
\end{enumerate}
CLF, however, lacked an explicit mechanism for a \emph{transient early enhancement} of $M_{\rm ind}^2$ to realize an EDE--like episode. CF--BWC supplies this.

\section{Condensate Formation and Boundary--Driven Wave Compression (CF--BWC)}
Consider an initial proto--vacuum fluid transitioning to a vacuum condensate. The conversion proceeds through an advancing interface with normal speed $u_{\rm f}$. In bulk, the fluid may be effectively incompressible (constant density), yet it supports waves (phase, pressure, density excitations). Waves encountering the boundary cannot outrun it if the component of their group velocity along the normal is smaller than $u_{\rm f}$; they are then \emph{compressed} (blue--shifted). The local fluctuation energy density increases within a boundary layer of thickness set by dissipation/healing length. This is the cosmological analogue of wave pileup near moving phase fronts in condensed matter.

\section{Induced Gravity Response: From Fluctuation Compression to EH Enhancement}
Induced--gravity theory implies that even absent a bare EH term, integrating out fluctuations generates
\be
S_{\rm ind}[g] \supset \int\! \dx^4 x\, \sqrt{-g}\;\frac{M_{\rm ind}^2}{2}\,R, 
\qquad M_{\rm ind}^2 \sim \sum_i \frac{N_i}{16\pi^2}\,\Lambda_{i,{\rm eff}}^2,
\ee
where $i$ labels fluctuation species and $\Lambda_{i,{\rm eff}}$ encodes the short--wavelength content of the spectrum \cite{Sakharov1967,Vassilevich2003}. Boundary--driven wave compression temporarily \emph{raises} $\Lambda_{i,{\rm eff}}$ and the occupancy of short modes, hence \emph{increasing} $M_{\rm ind}^2$ over a narrow epoch. The effective gravitational coupling becomes
\be
\Geff(a)=\frac{c^4}{8\pi\big[M_{\rm ind,0}^2+\Delta M_{\rm ind}^2(a)\big]},
\ee
with $M_{\rm ind,0}^2$ the late--time value, and $\Delta M_{\rm ind}^2(a)$ a positive, localized bump.

\section{Cosmological Mapping: A Minimal, EDE--like Parameterization}
A convenient, data--facing form for the transient enhancement is a lognormal in $\ln a$:
\be
\frac{\Delta M_{\rm ind}^2}{M_{\rm ind,0}^2}(a)=
A\,\exp\!\Big[-\frac{\ln^2(a/\ac)}{2\sigma^2}\Big], \qquad
\Geff(a)=\frac{c^4}{8\pi\left[M_{\rm ind,0}^2\left(1+A e^{-\ln^2(a/\ac)/(2\sigma^2)}\right)\right]},
\label{eq:Geffbump}
\ee
with peak amplitude $A>0$, center $\ac$ (typically $\zc\sim 3000$--$5000$), and narrow width $\sigma\lesssim 0.2$ in $\ln a$. Equivalently, one may encode the interface compression as a short--lived effective energy reservoir $u_{\rm mech}(a)$ that boosts $H(a)$ directly and decays rapidly:
\be
H^2(a) = \frac{8\pi G_0}{3}\left[\rho_{\rm std}(a)+u_{\rm mech}(a)\right], \quad
u_{\rm mech}(a)=\rho_{\rm c0}\,f_{\rm mech}
\exp\!\Big[-\frac{\ln^2(a/\ac)}{2\sigma^2}\Big].
\ee
Both forms realize the same physics: a localized early increase in the expansion rate.

\section{Effect on the Sound Horizon and the CMB--Inferred $H_0$}
The comoving sound horizon is
\be
\rs = \int_0^{a_\ast} \frac{\cs(a)}{a^2 H(a)}\,\dx a,
\qquad
\cs(a) = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3\left(1+\tfrac{3}{4}\rho_b/\rho_\gamma\right)}}.
\ee
A bump that raises $H(a)$ near $\ac$ reduces $\rs$ (denominator increases in the relevant window). CMB peak angles then imply a higher inferred $H_0$ once the model is fit to data, just as in EDE \cite{EDEReview2023,PoulinReview2023,Planck2018}. Because CF--BWC produces a \emph{short} and \emph{small} enhancement (few--to--ten percent), it can shift $H_0$ by $\mathcal{O}(5\%)$ while remaining consistent with other observables.

\section{CF--BWC as the Physical Engine of the Condensate Link Framework}
\subsection*{(i) Completing the mechanical--to--geometric matching}
The CLF equates mechanical stiffness of the condensate to geometric stiffness of spacetime. CF--BWC furnishes the \emph{process} that modulates stiffness: a phase boundary compresses fluctuations, increasing the one--loop induced EH coefficient temporarily. This supplies the exact, time--localized deviation the CLF needs to be cosmologically predictive.

\subsection*{(ii) Correct directionality for the $H_0$ shift}
Because compression \emph{increases} short--mode content, $M_{\rm ind}^2$ increases and $\Geff$ \emph{decreases} during the event; the curvature term strengthens, raising $H(a)$ pre--recombination. This is the required direction to \emph{shrink} $\rs$ and \emph{raise} the CMB--inferred $H_0$, aligning CLF with the EDE lever arm \cite{EDEReview2023,PoulinReview2023}.

\subsection*{(iii) Natural transience and bounded magnitude}
The energy available is bounded by the phase--conversion latent/free energy and interface kinematics ($u_{\rm f}$), making the enhancement short--lived and small. This naturally enforces the narrow width and few--percent amplitude demanded by BBN and CMB constraints \cite{Planck2018,Alvey2020}. Once the boundary vanishes, the spectrum relaxes and $M_{\rm ind}^2 \to M_{\rm ind,0}^2$, restoring standard GR at late times.

\subsection*{(iv) Compatibility with gravitational wave propagation}
The modification arises from scalar--sector fluctuations tied to the boundary epoch and does not persist. Hence the present--day tensor propagation speed remains $c$, in agreement with GW170817/GRB\,170817A \cite{GW170817}. CF--BWC therefore preserves the sharp bound $|c_T/c-1|\lesssim 10^{-15}$ at late times.

\subsection*{(v) A condensed--matter analogue that grounds the CLF}
Wave pileup at moving phase boundaries is a known feature of nonrelativistic media; the CF--BWC mapping to induced gravity provides a physically transparent analogue that anchors CLF's ``stiffness matching'' in a real microphysical process.

\section{Consistency Conditions and Practical Priors}
\paragraph{BBN.} Enforce that the enhancement is negligible at $T\!\sim\!\mathrm{MeV}$ (\emph{e.g.}, center at $\zc\sim 3000$ with sufficiently narrow $\sigma$ and small amplitude). Empirically $|\Delta H/H|_{\rm BBN}\lesssim\mathrm{few}\%$ suffices \cite{Alvey2020}.

\paragraph{CMB (Planck/ACT).} The bump must be narrow/early to avoid distorting the damping tail and the acoustic phases beyond current limits, while still shrinking $\rs$ \cite{Planck2018,EDEReview2023,PoulinReview2023}.

\paragraph{Late--time gravity and GW170817.} Enforce $\Geff(a\!=\!1)=G_0$ and vanishing tails at $z\!\ll\!10^3$; keep $c_T=c$ today \cite{GW170817}.

\section{Data--Facing Parameter Set and Test Plan}
A minimal parameter set is $\{\zc,\sigma,A\}$ for the induced--coefficient form in Eq.\,\eqref{eq:Geffbump}, or $\{\zc,\sigma,f_{\rm mech}\}$ for the effective reservoir. Profile--likelihood or Bayesian scans over Planck TT/TE/EE+lensing, BAO, Pantheon+/SNe, and SH0ES can be used to assess the allowed window and the achievable shift in $H_0$ \cite{Planck2018,RiessReview2024}. Weak--lensing ($S_8$) can be examined for secondary impacts.

\section{Conclusions}
We have completed the Condensate Link Framework by identifying a concrete microphysical engine---\emph{Condensate Formation with Boundary--Driven Wave Compression}---that briefly increases the induced gravitational stiffness in the early Universe. The resulting, EDE--like boost to the pre--recombination expansion rate shrinks $\rs$ and raises the CMB--inferred $H_0$, while the natural transience of the boundary process preserves BBN, CMB, and late--time constraints, including GW170817. CF--BWC thereby upgrades the CLF from a qualitative stiffness--matching picture to a quantitatively testable cosmological model.
