This review surveys the physics literature on compact objects that could constitute dark matter while being "dark" (non-luminous) but distinct from classical black holes with singularities. The research spans several theoretical frameworks:

7. **Exotic Compact Objects (ECOs)** in gravitational wave astronomy
8. **Loop Quantum Gravity Planck Stars** (Rovelli & Vidotto)
9. **String Theory Fuzzballs**

Each is analyzed for its relationship to the **frozen core concept**: regions at maximum density where the stiff equation of state (P = rho, c_s = c) leads to acoustic opacity--darkness arising from impedance mismatch rather than gravitational light-trapping.

## 1. Dark Stars Powered by Dark Matter Annihilation

### Key Papers

**Spolyar, D., Freese, K., & Gondolo, P. (2008)**
*"Dark Matter and the First Stars: A New Phase of Stellar Evolution"*
Physical Review Letters 100, 051101
arXiv: 0705.0521

**Freese, K., Bodenheimer, P., Spolyar, D., & Gondolo, P. (2008)**
*"Dark Matter Densities During the Formation of the First Stars and in Dark Stars"*
Astrophysical Journal 693, 1563
arXiv: 0807.3769

### Key Concept

These "dark stars" are hydrogen-helium stars powered by **WIMP dark matter annihilation** rather than nuclear fusion. They form in the early universe where dark matter density is high enough that annihilation heating overwhelms all cooling mechanisms.

**Properties:**
- Size: ~1-10 AU (extremely puffy)
- Surface temperature: ~10,000 K (cool)
- Can grow to supermassive scales: up to 10^7 solar masses
- Powered by ~1% dark matter by mass, but dark matter provides dominant energy source
- Eventually exhaust dark matter fuel and collapse to black holes

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | Dark Stars (Freese) | Frozen Cores |
|--------|---------------------|--------------|
| **Source of "darkness"** | Low surface temperature; emit some light but are dim | Acoustic opacity; no phonon propagation |
| **Support mechanism** | Dark matter annihilation heating | Stiff equation of state at maximum density |
| **Composition** | Ordinary matter (H, He) + trace dark matter | Pure BEC at ρ_stiff = c²/(8πG) |
| **Internal structure** | Extended, low-density, hot | Ultra-compact, maximum density |
| **Fate** | Collapse to black hole | Stable frozen configuration |

**Key Difference:** Freese-type dark stars are **luminous** (just dim) and represent a transient phase powered by dark matter annihilation. Frozen cores would be **acoustically opaque** (dark by impedance mismatch) and represent a fundamental ground state.

## 2. Gravastars (Gravitational Vacuum Stars)

### Key Papers

**Mazur, P.O. & Mottola, E. (2001/2004)**
*"Gravitational Condensate Stars: An Alternative to Black Holes"*
arXiv: gr-qc/0109035
Later published: Universe 9, 88 (2023)

**Visser, M. & Wiltshire, D.L. (2004)**
*"Stable Gravastars - An Alternative to Black Holes?"*
Classical and Quantum Gravity 21, 1135
arXiv: gr-qc/0310107

### Key Concept

A gravastar has three distinct regions:
1. **Interior:** de Sitter space (dark energy, P = -ρ)
2. **Shell:** Ultra-thin layer of stiff matter (P = +ρ)
3. **Exterior:** Schwarzschild vacuum

The interior is "gravitational vacuum" with equation of state P = -ρ (dark energy). The shell at the would-be horizon is composed of **stiff matter** with P = +ρ.

**Properties:**
- No singularity
- No event horizon (marginally trapped surface instead)
- Externally indistinguishable from a black hole
- Interior filled with dark energy
- Stable under perturbations (under certain conditions)

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | Gravastar (Mazur-Mottola) | Frozen Cores |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------|
| **Interior EOS** | P = -ρ (de Sitter/dark energy) | P = +ρ (stiff matter) |
| **Shell** | P = +ρ stiff matter | Not applicable (uniform) |
| **Support mechanism** | Dark energy repulsion + shell tension | Stiff EOS causal limit |
| **Size** | Approximately Schwarzschild radius | Scale-dependent on formation |
| **Interior condition** | Vacuum energy | Maximum BEC density |

**Key Similarity:** Both involve the **stiff equation of state** (P = ρ). In gravastars, stiff matter forms a thin shell. The frozen core concept proposes stiff matter as the **entire interior** state.

**Key Difference:** Gravastar interiors are dark energy (negative pressure), while frozen cores would have positive pressure at the causal limit. Gravastars require exotic shell matter; frozen cores would be homogeneous BEC at maximum density.

**Important Connection:** The gravastar shell's P = +ρ stiff matter is precisely what characterizes the frozen core interior. One could view frozen cores as "all shell, no de Sitter interior."

## 3. Black Stars (Semiclassical Gravity Alternative)

### Key Papers

**Barcelo, C., Liberati, S., Sonego, S., & Visser, M. (2008)**
*"Fate of Gravitational Collapse in Semiclassical Gravity"*
Physical Review D 77, 044032

**Barcelo, C., Liberati, S., Sonego, S., & Visser, M. (2009)**
*"Black Stars, Not Holes"*
Scientific American (October 2009)

**Visser, M. et al. (2009)**
*"Small, Dark, and Heavy: But is it a Black Hole?"*
Proceedings (available on arXiv)

### Key Concept

Black stars arise from semiclassical gravity effects:
- As a star collapses, vacuum polarization builds up near the would-be horizon
- This creates a negative stress-energy tensor from quantum effects
- The collapse **halts** before horizon formation
- Result: An ultra-compact object with no singularity and no true event horizon

**Key Mechanism:** Space itself provides the support through quantum vacuum effects. The black star is "frozen" just outside where the horizon would form.

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | Black Star (Barcelo et al.) | Frozen Cores |
|--------|----------------------------|--------------|
| **Halting mechanism** | Vacuum polarization / quantum effects | Stiff EOS causal limit |
| **Framework** | Semiclassical gravity (QFT in curved space) | BEC hydrodynamics |
| **Location of "freeze"** | Just outside Schwarzschild radius | At ρ_stiff density |
| **Observational appearance** | Extremely dark, gravitationally redshifted | Acoustically opaque |
| **Time dilation** | Extreme but finite | Determined by local curvature |

**Key Similarity:** Both represent **frozen collapse** - the star stops short of singularity formation. Both would appear essentially identical to black holes from far away.

**Key Difference:** Black stars rely on quantum field theory effects (vacuum polarization) in curved spacetime. Frozen cores would arise from classical BEC hydrodynamics reaching the causal limit of the equation of state.

## 4. Dark Energy Stars (Chapline-Laughlin Model)

### Key Papers

**Chapline, G. (2005)**
*"Dark Energy Stars"*
Proceedings of the 22nd Texas Symposium on Relativistic Astrophysics
arXiv: astro-ph/0503200

**Chapline, G., Hohlfeld, E., Laughlin, R.B., & Santiago, D.I. (2000)**
*"Quantum Phase Transitions and the Breakdown of Classical General Relativity"*
(Early formulation of BEC vacuum model)

### Key Concept

Based on modeling spacetime as a **Bose-Einstein condensate**, Chapline and Laughlin propose:
- The event horizon represents a **quantum critical phase transition** in the vacuum
- Infalling matter converts to dark energy at this surface
- No singularity forms; interior is stabilized by dark energy pressure
- Black holes do not exist - only dark energy stars

**Connection to Superfluidity:** The model draws direct analogy to quantum critical phase transitions in superfluids and superconductors.

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | Dark Energy Star (Chapline) | Frozen Cores |
|--------|----------------------------|--------------|
| **Theoretical basis** | BEC vacuum / emergent spacetime | BEC cosmology |
| **Phase transition** | At horizon surface | At maximum density |
| **Interior** | Dark energy (P = -ρ) | Stiff BEC (P = +ρ) |
| **Matter fate** | Converts to vacuum energy | Remains as frozen BEC |
| **Prediction** | Positron emission from surface | Acoustic opacity |

**Key Similarity:** Both are based on **BEC physics** and view spacetime/matter as a condensate. Both propose phase transitions as central to the physics.

**Key Difference:** In Chapline's model, matter is destroyed (converted to dark energy). In the frozen core model, matter persists but in a maximally dense, acoustically opaque state. The equations of state have opposite signs.

- Early universe may have had a stiff matter phase (P = ρ)
- Sound speed equals light speed
- Energy density scales as a^-6
- Can arise from BEC dark matter with specific interactions

**Key Similarity:** The BEC dark matter literature provides the mathematical framework (Gross-Pitaevskii equation, polytropic EOS) that could underlie frozen cores. The "frozen states" in recent papers (2026) are directly relevant.

### Key Concept

Fuzzy dark matter (FDM) / ultralight axion dark matter:
- Particle mass: ~10^-22 eV
- de Broglie wavelength: ~1 kpc (galactic scale!)
- Forms stable **soliton cores** in halo centers
- Interference patterns from wave nature
- Solves cusp-core problem of CDM

**Soliton Properties:**
- Ground state of Schrodinger-Poisson equation
- Flat-density core with r^-3 envelope
- Mass scales inversely with radius
- Stable over cosmological timescales

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | FDM Soliton Cores | Frozen Cores |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Scale** | ~kpc (galactic core size) | Much smaller (compact) |
| **Density** | ~10^-25 g/cm³ | Maximum: ρ_stiff ~ 10^94 g/cm³ |
| **Support** | Quantum pressure (uncertainty) | Stiff EOS limit |
| **Wave nature** | de Broglie waves | Phonon modes frozen |
| **Darkness** | Transparent (weakly interacting) | Acoustically opaque |

**Key Difference:** FDM solitons are large, diffuse, and transparent. Frozen cores would be ultra-compact and opaque. They operate at opposite ends of the density spectrum.

**Potential Connection:** If FDM solitons could undergo further collapse, the frozen core concept suggests they would reach a maximum density state rather than a singularity. However, FDM's quantum pressure normally prevents such collapse.

| **Reflectivity** | Model-dependent | High (impedance mismatch) |

**Key Opportunity:** The frozen core hypothesis predicts a specific type of ECO with:
- Surface determined by acoustic opacity, not quantum/exotic physics

## 8. Loop Quantum Gravity: Planck Stars

### Key Concept

In loop quantum gravity:
- Collapsing matter reaches **Planck density** (~10^93 g/cm³)
- Quantum gravity effects create repulsion at this scale
- Black hole "bounces" into a white hole
- Intermediate state is a "Planck star"

**Properties:**
- Maximum density at Planck scale (not infinite)
- No singularity
- Quantum tunneling to white hole on long timescales
- All information preserved

### Relation to Frozen Cores

| Aspect | Planck Star (LQG) | Frozen Cores |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Maximum density** | Planck density ~10^93 g/cm³ | ρ_stiff = c²/(8πG) ~ 10^94 g/cm³ |
| **Mechanism** | Quantum gravity repulsion | Classical causal EOS limit |
| **Framework** | Loop quantum gravity | BEC hydrodynamics |
| **Fate** | Bounces to white hole | Stable frozen state |
| **Timescale** | Tunnels on ~M³ timescale | Permanently frozen |

**Remarkable Numerical Coincidence:** The Planck density (~10^93 g/cm³) and the stiff matter density ρ_stiff = c²/(8πG) (~10^94 g/cm³) are within an order of magnitude!

**Key Similarity:** Both posit a **maximum density** that halts collapse before singularity formation. Both propose physics at ~10^93-94 g/cm³.

**Key Difference:** Planck stars are quantum gravity objects that eventually tunnel to white holes. Frozen cores would be classically stable BEC configurations. The Planck star is a transient; the frozen core would be permanent.

## 11. Acoustic Analogues and Dumb Holes

### Key Papers

**Unruh, W.G. (1981)**
*"Experimental Black Hole Evaporation?"*
Physical Review Letters 46, 1351

**Unruh, W.G. (1995)**
*"Sonic Analogue of Black Holes and the Effects of High Frequencies on Black Hole Evaporation"*
Physical Review D 51, 2827

**Barcelo, C., Liberati, S., & Visser, M. (2005)**
*"Analogue Gravity"*
Living Reviews in Relativity 8, 12

### Key Concept

Acoustic black holes ("dumb holes") arise when:
- Fluid flow exceeds local sound speed
- Phonons cannot escape from supersonic region
- Creates acoustic horizon analogous to event horizon
- Emits thermal Hawking-like radiation

**Experimental:** First acoustic black hole created in BEC in 2009; Hawking radiation analogue detected.

### Critical Relevance to Frozen Cores

The **acoustic/dumb hole** literature is perhaps the most directly relevant to the frozen core hypothesis:

| Aspect | Dumb Hole (Unruh) | Frozen Core |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Mechanism** | Flow exceeds sound speed | Sound speed equals c (limit) |
| **Horizon type** | Sonic horizon | Acoustic opacity surface |
| **Trapped excitation** | Phonons | Phonons |
| **Laboratory analogue** | Supersonic BEC flow | Extremely dense BEC |
| **Hawking analogue** | Yes (thermal phonons) | Unclear (limiting case) |

**Key Insight:** Dumb holes trap phonons by having flow > sound speed. Frozen cores would trap phonons by having reached the **maximum possible sound speed** (c). In both cases, information in phonon modes becomes inaccessible.

**Important Distinction:** In a dumb hole, phonons exist but cannot escape. In a frozen core at P = ρ, the question is whether phonon modes can propagate at all when c_s = c. This is the **causal limit** of acoustic propagation.
